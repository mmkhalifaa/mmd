05The organizing principle

Four ones.

One client
One living memory
One coordinated team
One action state
Everything that follows is this sentence, worn differently — for the banker, the specialist, the analyst, the leader, and the client.
i
The center

Every client has an always-on Client Agent.

A persistent, permissioned agent that holds everything we are authorized to understand: the family and its entities, goals, portfolios, liabilities, preferences, past conversations, important decisions, open opportunities — and every commitment we have made. Not a better CRM record. A living memory of the relationship.

It is also the relationship’s chief of staff: investment, lending, planning, and service agents coordinated around one shared understanding of the client — one relationship plan, clear ownership, no conflicting outreach, and every promise tracked until it is done.

iThe center · What it holds

A living memory, in four verbs.

It remembers.
Family, entities, goals, portfolios, liabilities, preferences, decisions, open questions — and every commitment made anywhere in the relationship.
It senses.
Permitted signals from meetings, calls, email, transactions, service activity, and markets — what changed, what may matter, what needs attention.
It asks.
Facts, data, judgment, signals, and AI assumptions stay distinct. When unsure: “We believe this may be becoming a priority for the client. Is that correct?”
It coordinates.
One plan. Clear ownership. No duplicated or conflicting outreach. The action state lives in one place, visible until complete.
Smarter every week — through partnership with the people who know the relationship best.
ii
The flagship

The smartest room in private banking.

Clients come to us for decisions that are important, complex, and deeply personal — selling a business, moving wealth to the children, changing the risk in a portfolio, bringing the family into one plan. In those moments they need more than information. They need judgment, trust, and confidence that the right people are thinking with them.

The office becomes a decision environment clients cannot reproduce on their own — their complete context, the firm’s proprietary intelligence, its best specialists, trusted human judgment, and a safe path from conversation to execution. The banker leads. The room thinks.

iiThe flagship · Sixty minutes in the room

The meeting, replayed.

Before. The Client Agent has prepared the team — goals, plan, lending, unresolved questions, what changed — and asked the advisor to confirm anything uncertain. The client has already shaped the agenda.
In the room. “We may sell part of the business next year — we worry about liquidity, income, and the risk we carry elsewhere.” The banker asks the room. Ownership, exposures, and scenarios appear — and evolve as the client changes an assumption.
Deeper. The right specialist anywhere in the firm joins — in person, virtually, or through their approved expertise as an agent. The client never needs to understand how the firm is organized to get the best of it.
Before they leave. Decisions separated from ideas, follow-ups prepared, approved next steps already moving — and a secure recap and workspace to continue at home.
Explicitly permissioned
Clients see when AI is active, what it uses, and what is retained. Anyone can pause it, exclude a topic, or correct the record — and every analysis shows its sources, assumptions, and whether it is an approved calculation, a professional view, or a hypothesis for review.
iii
Full command

Our best people, running always-on teams.

One command surface in Coach — potentially no more than a large text box — and a live dashboard of only what matters now. State the outcome: a plan, a portfolio analysis, a trade, a lending structure, research across the whole book. The platform builds the analysis, the interface, or the temporary workspace the task needs.

“Brian called me about this. Monitor these conditions — if they occur, analyze the implications, prepare the response, and bring it back to me.” From that point forward, the agent keeps working. Another watches the inbox: low-risk requests completed within boundaries, the rest returned with context, evidence, and approvals ready.

iv
Autopilot

Every advisor, closer to the best.

“Three relationships to focus on this morning. Here is what changed, why it matters, what our specialists think — and the work I have already prepared.”
— Autopilot, before the open.

It reads calendars, conversations, portfolios, markets, and outstanding commitments, then surfaces the few actions most likely to matter — meetings prepared, the right investment, lending, or planning skill recommended, specific coaching after the conversations that count. Command and guided are not classifications of people. The same person takes full control where they are expert and more guidance where they are not. The ceiling rises for the best; the standard rises for everyone.

v
The machinery

From client intent to finished work.

An address change, a mortgage, a line of credit: the banker should never coordinate eleven applications by hand. An agent understands the intent, gathers what is required, coordinates the systems and specialists, tracks the work, and keeps everyone informed. Controlled systems keep identity, authorization, money movement, and books and records — the certainties stay certain.

Operations professionals become supervisors of intelligent execution — exceptions, judgment, and improving the workflows themselves. When something fails, an agent assembles the full history, explains the problem, and recommends resolutions before the expert steps in.

07The machinery · The operating model

From coordinating tasks to overseeing agents.

A line of credit today: the file moves down a queue, each step waiting on the one before it. Next, agents run the steps in parallel, and people approve at the checkpoints that matter.
Today: people in sequence — a handoff at every step. Days to weeks.

09Reinventing apprenticeship

The parallel book and the wind tunnel.

The parallel book
Clients who are not real
Every analyst runs simulated families — businesses, portfolios, inboxes, voices, personalities, evolving needs. They practice investments, lending, planning, the anxious call, the complex explanation, the recovery from a mistake. Alongside it, real apprenticeship on live relationships teaches trust, empathy, and accountability.
Simulation accelerates development. It does not replace real relationships.
The wind tunnel
Where the firm tests itself
The same synthetic environment becomes the proving ground: new agents, skills, products, and workflows run against realistic relationships before they ever touch production.
Nothing meets a real client untested.
10Power Friday
